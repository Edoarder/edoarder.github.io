Research interests

How do interactions between people, institutions, and information systems produce collective patterns? I approach this question through three connected strands of research.

01 Network structure & higher-order pathways 02 Digital traces, communication & behaviour 03 Social interaction & simulation
01

Network structure & higher-order pathways

How do indirect connections change the way we understand a social system?

I study how network structure shapes possible pathways of attention and interaction. My work combines statistically validated community reconstruction, random walks, and network motifs to compare direct connections with multi-step patterns within and between groups. A second strand uses multilayer representations to compare communication channels and identify structural roles across them.

02

Digital traces, communication & behaviour

What can online communication tell us about collective attention and action?

I combine digital traces with surveys and official statistics to study how online communication relates to behaviour and social context. My research links climate discourse with regional pro-environmental behaviour, and examines the communication infrastructures surrounding energy governance. I develop interpretable indicators and statistical analyses that connect large, heterogeneous datasets to substantive social questions.

03

Social interaction & simulation

How do individual relations give rise to collective patterns?

My work on signed social networks uses agent-based modelling to explore the interplay between social interaction, structural balance, and geometrical frustration. Developed during my research at ETH Zürich, this model connects local relational rules with network-level outcomes through simulation and parameter exploration. It grounds my broader interest in collective behaviour and empirically informed social modelling.

Methods

Representing social systems

Network reconstruction and statistical validation; community detection; multilayer and signed networks.

Connecting heterogeneous data

Social-media traces, Eurobarometer surveys, Eurostat indicators, regional harmonisation, and NLP.

Analysing collective patterns

Random walks and motifs; regression and robustness analysis; agent-based simulation and parameter exploration.

Future directions

I am interested in how the structure of social networks changes over time, and in what we gain by retaining different types of relationships in a multilayer representation.

Related directions include comparisons with null models, empirically grounded agent-based models, synthetic populations and networks, and the use of computational evidence in sustainability and public policy.
